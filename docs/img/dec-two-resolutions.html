The URL index is stored at two resolutions, and the coarse one is derived

The readers ask at exactly two resolutions: the whole retention window, and the last complete hour. Five-minute precision buys the window's recent EDGE and nothing behind it, so behind the edge the index is read as hours, from a coarse key the writer folds out of the fine buckets.

One shard's read plan across a 24-hour window: Stats_Store::read_plan() splits it into { fine, hours }

Anything further back is the coarse tier's alone. An hour with no key there has not been folded YET, and an EMPTY hour is still written, so absence means exactly one thing. Both tiers hold the same row shape on purpose: one fold_index_row() reads both, one merge_url_row() writes both.
No coarse key yet? Only the hour right behind the fine tail is answered from its twelve fine buckets, whatever their age (unfolded_hour_buckets()). A fresh deploy, or the hour a worker restarted through, self-heals instead of leaving a hole.
FINE_BUCKETS (13) is a FLOOR: the tail runs to the END of the hour it lands in. Half fine and half coarse is counted twice or read at NEITHER resolution, a hole of up to eleven buckets that breathes with the minute hand and silently shrinks every count on the page.

The fold runs on the writer's flush path

Not on newspack_nodes/periodic: the cron pass is the documented home for minute-or-slower derived housekeeping, but persist_aggregate_stats() rests on one partition having one writer, and a cron process folding p{N}'s keys puts a second one in a keyspace the substrate's routing exists to keep single-writer. Idempotence would make that benign; the invariant is worth more than the placement.

The fold is idempotent because it OVERWRITES from the fine buckets; adding into an hour would double-count every re-flush. The fine buckets outlive their fold, and a folded hour's buckets are never read again. It stays PER SHARD: each shard carries its own Other overflow row, and a merge keyed on the url_hash would collapse sixteen of them into one.

Why the coarse tier is not mirrored

STATS_MIRROR_TOPN excludes urls_h: it is derived from urls, which is mirrored in full and retained for <eln:stats_mirror_lifetime>, twice the stats window, so a fine bucket long expired from memcache is still on disk and the rehydrate seam rebuilds an evicted hour from it. The fold cannot, because roll_up_hours() reads memcache; a reader can, and that asymmetry is what the exclusion rests on. A durable copy would store the same information twice on the axis decision 11 watches, and an hour frame would be the largest thing in a checkpoint's held set.

The grace hour is read from ALL twelve buckets whatever their age against ttl_url_fine(): that TTL is the memcache lifetime, and the reader is not memcache-only.

Not a scope, a resolution

The coarse key does not reopen decision 1: the bucket stays LAST, one lookup_multi serves the tier, and retention is still the key's own TTL. A resolution is a constant this schema chooses, exactly as the shard count is, and not an input it does not control.

Decision 6 is unharmed by chunking: the batch is still one round trip for many keys, bounded to a chunk. What exhausts the heap is the volume resident at once, not a duplicate copy.
