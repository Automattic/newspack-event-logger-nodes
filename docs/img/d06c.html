The life of a five-minute bucket

Stats_Store writes one memcache key per partition, per namespace, per five-minute bucket, 288 to a day. A flush folds each closed hour of the URL index into a coarse tier, and every write is shadowed to disk. Three tiers, three lifetimes.

The dashboard reads the last hour in buckets and the rest of the window in hours: 36 to 46 keys per shard instead of 288.
The open hour is not folded yet. A flush folds the URL index's closed hour, at most once every five seconds.
A bucket's frames are written at the first checkpoint after it closes. Until then they ride the consumer's offsetlog, capped at two megabytes per checkpoint.
Frames are kept for twice the statistics window, so the mirror reaches back past it.
Each key expires on its own, so retention is its time to live, floored at an hour.

What a key holds

Sums and counts, never means, so buckets and partitions merge by addition and the dashboard divides at read time.

Caps hold each value under memcache's one-megabyte limit: 50 categories, 20 dimension values and 2,000 URLs per shard of the URL index. The overflow folds into an Other row, so totals stay exact.

A miss falls through to the mirror

The read lands the frame back in memcache, within 1,500 milliseconds per verb. The hourly tiers are not mirrored, because the buckets they fold from are.

Rotates the install's salt and orphans every key at once. The mirror's keys carry no install scope, so it is the one tier the rotation cannot reach.
