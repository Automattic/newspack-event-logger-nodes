Every stat lives under one key shape, and the bucket is always the last component

Memcache is where stats are written and read; the durable mirror of decisions 10 and 11 shadows them for read-back on a miss. Because every scope is a key PREFIX, one lookup_multi serves them all through Stats_Store::lookup_buckets(), retention is the key's own TTL rather than a hand-rolled cutoff pass, and whether a bucket is still open is decided from the key alone.

Three TTL roles, one setting

max_lifespan is a Stats_Store constructor property seeded from the substrate's min_lifetime (default 43,200) through Config::stats_retention_seconds(), floored at PREFIX_FLOOR = 3,600; it is not itself a config key on either side. A FINE urls or urlnames bucket gets its own read window, FINE_TTL_SECONDS, two hours, because the coarse tier answers for everything behind it: 24 buckets a shard, the same as the coarse tier's 24 hours; the per-URL blob gets a 24th; everything else gets the whole window.

The server ceiling is 128 rather than 20 because 20 binds on a real fleet: it folds every spoke past the twentieth into Other, and a 24-spoke fleet loses four sites from its own picker. Reading the axis as the fleet and leaving it uncapped is wrong: server_name is SERVER_NAME, under Apache's default UseCanonicalName Off the client's Host header, so a catch-all vhost makes it visitor input. Decision 14 owns the reopen condition. A shard is the one component naming no dimension of the data: physical partitioning, because one blob per bucket is the pressure every cap here is sized against.
