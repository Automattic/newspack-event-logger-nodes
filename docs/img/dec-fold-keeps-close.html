The fold keeps the close of any span the kept head left open

One job record from community.thecoast.ca, folded twice. The gyrobase subprocess opened in the kept head and closed after 535.8 s of a 536.2 s request; ten PHP rows followed it. Left: the close competes for the rolling tail and loses. Right: the fold routes it into the keep bucket, because the fold chose the head and knows which frames it left open.

Without the rule: the close is one more tail row

The entry list leaves gyrobase open, so the span adopts every row after it. The view reconstructs framing the record does not state, and three display defects follow. The flame stays right: Flame_Fold::close(), the only path that increments a node's count, reads gyrobase count 1, 535,829.753 ms.

With the rule: the fold keeps what it left open

head · FOLD_KEEP_HEAD = 10
process (start) … gyrobase (start) open_in_head() records it at the fold
entries (aggregated)the middle, merged into a Flame_Fold tree
keep · spliced between the marker and the tail
gyrobase (complete) closes_head_span() routes it here
tail · rolling FOLD_KEEP_TAIL = 10
nuclear_gyrobase query hook × 2 pairs updated_option hook memory · resources process (complete) exempt: the terminal must END the record
The record states its own framing. At most FOLD_KEEP_HEAD closes can land in keep, since that is how many frames a head can leave open. The request's own frame is exempt, as it is for the display's severed-span prune: keep lands BEFORE the tail, and process (complete) must be last.

Why this is not tail sizing

is_kept() argues that sizing the tail around a producer's shutdown sequence is guesswork the producer should settle by marking the line. A span's close is not that guess: the fold just chose the head, so it knows exactly which frames it left open.

Why the close is not detail

A span opened in the head FRAMES every row after it. Losing its (complete) costs the record its shape, not one line.
