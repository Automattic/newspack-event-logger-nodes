The per-URL percentiles are retired, and the duration reservoir with them

Every field the URL index keeps must survive a fold over N buckets, because the table sums the whole retention window. A percentile cannot: the window's p95 is no function of the buckets' p95s. The two honest ends are a mergeable sketch or deletion, and the sketch is priced in bytes.

Run-encoded at 1.5x bins, the histogram measures 71–167 B/row for 1–9% accuracy: up to 44% back onto the row, and more than the whole sole-server collapse saves. Counts ADD, so it would merge through sum_entry() with no new machinery. It is rejected on price alone: the bytes would make a number nobody sorts by more nearly true.

Not stored

ROW_P50_MS, ROW_P95_MS and ROW_P99_MS (38 B/row), ROW_DURATIONS, apply_percentiles(), MAX_DURATIONS_PER_BUCKET, and the whole NS_URL_DUR namespace, which spares the WRITE path up to 100 floats per row per bucket, a STATS_MIRROR_TOPN carve-out, and one lookup_multi per shard per hour fold.

Kept, and exact

min_ms and max_ms fold from duration_ms directly and cost two fields. The urls verb's slowest-ten ranks by avg_ms, a read-time division over merged sums; Findings::for_url() reports max_ms; Ask_Assembler carries both. On a low-count URL a mean is more volatile than a 100-sample p95, and max_ms is the steadier ranking if that shows.

Why no older row is read

A layout change moves indexes: ROW_SRV occupies the reservoir's index, and every index past 7 is one lower than a row carrying its URL string would use (decision 1 keeps it in urlmap). A row of an older layout would read its split at an index that now holds something else. Decision 5 governs: each release rotates the salt, so no such row is ever read. The unsalted OFFSETLOG checkpoint costs one worker's un-flushed per-server delta once, which restore_state() declares acceptable.
